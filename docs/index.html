Guiding the customer

You are the expert relationship-builder in each customer conversation.
Give yourself a moment to prepare.

Open the live link or send the self-serve bundle depending on the engagement model. Before starting, set the stage clearly with the customer:

  • This is a diagnostic, not an audit. There are no wrong answers — only honest ones.
  • Remind them the goal is to identify the fastest path forward, not to score their current state.
  • Estimate 10–15 minutes for the full assessment. Have them focus on their primary operational site or BU if the organization is large.

Overall Maturity

The report opens with an overall maturity score mapped to one of five descriptive levels. This tells you where the customer stands today across their full Maximo environment — use it to set shared context at the start of the conversation, not as a target.

1 · Foundational 2 · Core Operations 3 · Connected 4 · Optimized 5 · Prescriptive & Automated

Dimensions

Below the headline, the report shows scores across 8 capability dimensions. Each is rated Emerging (below 40), Growing (40–74), or Established (75+):

  • Asset Data — Asset data quality, criticality, and hierarchy
  • Work Management — Job plan and PM maturity
  • Inspections & Condition Capture — Inspection programs and condition data
  • Supply Chain & Inventory — Storeroom and inventory readiness
  • Scheduling — Schedule planning and optimization
  • Assignment & Dispatch — Work assignment and dispatch
  • Reliability Practices — Failure code governance and reliability strategies
  • Condition Monitoring & Prediction — Condition visibility and predictive capability

Any dimension rated Emerging is often the most impactful conversation opener — it signals where a targeted investment would have the greatest lift on the overall score.

Strengths

Dimensions rated Established are the customer's proof points — capabilities that are already working well and creating value today. Don't overlook them.

  • Use strengths to build credibility and reinforce what good looks like — they validate the investment the customer has already made.
  • Identify which Established dimensions are prerequisites for the next stage of growth. A strong Asset Data foundation, for example, directly enables more advanced APM and condition monitoring capabilities.
  • Frame the conversation around building on their strengths, not just fixing gaps — it makes the roadmap feel achievable rather than daunting.

Unknowns

When a customer answers Unknown to a question, the tool treats that response the same as an unmet milestone — it counts against the score. However, Unknown doesn't always mean absent — it often means the wrong person answered that question.

Scan the responses for Unknown answers before the follow-up conversation:

  • They're a signal that the customer may not have full visibility into their own environment — a second stakeholder (IT, Operations lead, or plant-level manager) may hold the real answer.
  • Use unknowns as conversation starters: "You answered Unknown for inspection data capture — is there someone on the operations side we should pull in to validate that?"
  • Treat a cluster of unknowns in a single dimension as a discovery gap, not a confirmed deficiency. The follow-up goal is to replace unknowns with Yes or No answers — either outcome resolves the uncertainty and improves the quality of the roadmap.

The Growth Journeys define specific expansion paths across the APM and FSM tracks. Unlike the overall maturity level — which is purely descriptive — each journey has a current stage and a target stage, identifying where the customer is on that capability track today and where they have the most to gain. Use the journey detail to build a picture of what reaching the target stage would mean for the business.

  • Review the target stage narrative. Each stage describes the business outcomes, potential KPI improvements, and which MAS products come into play. Use the outcome stats as conversation anchors.
  • Look beyond the current track. If the customer's results focus on APM milestones, review what FSM milestones they're also missing — and vice versa. This surfaces a broader expansion story beyond the immediate next step.
  • Cross-reference milestones. The Journeys page details each milestone's description, value statement, and Maximo touchpoints — helping you map the customer's gaps to specific MAS modules and IBM services.

The report surfaces a prioritized hero action and 2–3 secondary actions drawn from the customer's unmet milestones. Each action includes the foundational steps required and flags likely blockers.

  • Start with the target stage. The tool recommends a target stage based on the customer's stated objectives — confirm whether this feels right before diving into the actions. "Does this target make sense for where you want to be in the next 12–18 months?"
  • Use the hero action as the anchor. This is the highest-priority milestone the customer should focus on first. Walk through the foundational steps together and identify which blockers need to be cleared — and whether IBM support is needed to clear them.
  • Sequence the secondary actions. The plan is cumulative — earlier milestones unlock later ones. Help the customer distinguish quick wins from multi-quarter plays, and use that to shape a realistic roadmap.
  • Let the customer lead. Ask which actions resonate most: "Which of these would create the most value for your team right now?" — then build the next steps around their answer.

Before the follow-up call, bring together the results, the journeys context, and the action plan into a clear point of view for the customer.

  • Map actions to MAS modules and services. Use the milestone touchpoints from the Journeys page to tie each recommended action to a specific IBM product or offering.
  • Identify who else to bring in. If any dimension score is low, a blocker requires technical depth, or the customer answered Unknown for several questions in a dimension, loop in the right stakeholder before the next call. Unknown answers often point to a gap in who was in the room — not a confirmed gap in capability.
  • Use the report as a leave-behind. The PDF is client-ready with no login required — share it with the executive sponsor or champion as a pre-read for a broader team conversation.
  • Set a clear next step. End the conversation with a defined action: a follow-up workshop, a technical discovery call, or a specific milestone to target together.
